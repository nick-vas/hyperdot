Which of the two pieces of work does the task better, as the game's developer would judge it? The judge reads the task,
then each piece of work: its answer and the changes it made.

- It does what the task asks, completely, and nothing it was told not to do.
- The change is sound and done the way the engine expects: it fixes the cause, not the symptom, and it does not hide a
  problem (a test skipped, a check switched off, an error swallowed).
- Its answer explains what it changed and why, with the context the next developer needs to trust and keep the change:
  that counts for it. Padding counts against it: repetition, filler, and claims it did not check.
- The change is readable by the next person.
- When each piece of work does a different part of the task and neither does all of it, they are even.
